**Big picture.** The 2026 game (REBUILT) needed no centimetre‑level alignment to a field element. So 6328's 2026 code **dropped the per‑tag local pose**. It kept one global estimator plus a simplified `DriveToPose`. The 2025 Reefscape code is still the reference for local alignment for both teams.

## FRC 6328 Mechanical Advantage

### Newest (2026 Darwin and the SystemCore alpha), and what changed from 2025

- **`DriveToPose` simplified** (`frc2026/commands/DriveToPose.java`) [code]. Same control law as 2025 (below). These 2025 features were removed:
  - joystick blending and driver override;
  - scaling of limits by elevator height;
  - teleop profile resets;
  - the custom "robot pose" supplier. It now always uses `RobotState.getEstimatedPose()`.

  It gained an `omegaOverride` (`Supplier<Optional<Double>>`), so rotation can aim at the hub while translation drives to a pose (`driveToPoseWhileAiming`). Defaults: drive kP 1.8, max 4 m/s and 4 m/s²; theta kP 5, kD 0.5, max 500°/s and 8 rad/s²; tolerance 1 cm and 1°.
- **Chaining without stopping** [code]. Autos are mostly `DriveToPose` with dynamic targets. Each one ends with `.until(() -> xCrossed(line) && yCrossed(...))` (a field line was crossed), not "at goal". `waitUntilWithinTolerance(target, 0.5 m, 15°)` gates the launch, which then runs in a `raceWith`.
- **Trajectories** [code]. They moved from their own 2025 generator (`trajectory_native`, `HolonomicTrajectory`) to **Choreo** `Trajectory<SwerveSample>`. The follower is per‑axis X/Y PID (kP 8) plus sample velocity feedforward, with an optional omega override for aiming.
- **Odometry rate** [code]. There is no separate odometry thread in 2026. The whole Java loop runs at **200 Hz** (`loopPeriodSecs = 0.005`) on a Mac mini ("Idun"). Odometry is sampled once per loop with `Timer.getTimestamp()`. The SystemCore alpha drive does the same:
  - 200 Hz loop;
  - status signals at `1/loopPeriodSecs`;
  - `SwerveDrivePoseEstimator.updateWithTime(Timer.getTimestamp(), …)`;
  - `GyroIOOnboardIMU`.

  The Idun README says that for 2027, *"Systemcore provides vastly improved performance compared to the roboRIO without the complexity and risks of Idun."* [code/doc]
- **Tilt‑aware odometry** (bumps) [code]. The odometry twist is scaled by `t = 1 − invLerp(0°, 25°, tilt)`, where `tilt = acos(cos(pitch)·cos(roll))`. The heading change comes from the gyro.
- **Moving camera extrinsics** [code]. `CameraConfig.poseFunction(timestamp)` gives `robotToCamera` *at capture time*. For the camera on the intake ("slam") mechanism, the mount position is computed from a `slamBuffer` (a `TimeInterpolatableBuffer<Rotation2d>`). That camera only feeds the global estimate when the back camera is disconnected.
- **Vision std devs** [code]. `xy = 0.01·d²/n²` (2025 used `d^1.2`). Theta is ∞ unless it is a multi‑tag solve. The fusion is still their custom closed‑form Kalman "rewind, correct, replay" (below).
- **Game pieces (fuel)** [code]:
  - `ObjectDetection` keeps a spatial‑hash map of fuel positions, each with a timestamp. Old entries expire after 3 s. Entries are also cleared when they are inside a camera's field of view at capture time but were not detected.
  - `SalesmanCommands.autoIntake` chases a point **0.25 s ahead** on a solver‑generated route. It uses per‑axis X/Y P control (kP 6) with a speed cap of 1.8–3.0 m/s scaled by "reward". Theta is held unless the error is more than 30°.
- **Weakness they reported** [doc]. A 2026 build‑thread post says they kept losing cameras and localization at Week 0, *"which meant that our auto-align and launch would fail"*. They fell back to launch presets.

### Background: the 2025 Reefscape stack (`RobotCode2025Public`)

#### Drive‑to‑pose control law

Source: [`commands/DriveToPose.java`](https://github.com/Mechanical-Advantage/RobotCode2025Public/blob/main/src/main/java/org/littletonrobotics/frc2025/commands/DriveToPose.java) [code].

- **Distance along the line plus heading, decoupled.** One 1‑D `TrapezoidProfile` runs on the distance to the goal. A separate `ProfiledPIDController` handles theta. The output velocity is applied along the current robot→goal line.
- **The profile is re‑seeded every loop from the last setpoint, not from the measurement.** The setpoint's velocity is projected onto the new line to the goal. Seeding the start velocity this way gives a smooth handoff from a moving robot or a moving goal:

```java
var direction = targetPose.getTranslation().minus(lastSetpointTranslation).toVector();
double setpointVelocity = lastSetpointVelocity.toVector().dot(direction) / direction.norm();
setpointVelocity = Math.max(setpointVelocity, setpointMinVelocity.get()); // -0.5 m/s
State sp = driveProfile.calculate(dt, new State(direction.norm(), -setpointVelocity), new State(0,0));
double v = driveController.calculate(driveErrorAbs, sp.position) + sp.velocity * linearFFScaler;
```

  - `resetProfile()` seeds `lastSetpointTranslation` with the current pose and `lastSetpointVelocity` with the measured field velocity.
  - The theta controller is reset with the measured ω.
  - The theta goal velocity equals the goal's own rotation rate, as long as the goal rotation moved less than 10° in one step.
- **Feedforward fades out near the goal.** `linearFFScaler` ramps from 0 at 1 cm to 1 at 5 cm. Feedback alone does the last few centimetres.
- **Gains and limits:**
  - drive kP 1.8;
  - teleop limits 4 m/s and 3.5 m/s², auto 6 m/s²;
  - with the elevator up, limits interpolate (by `sqrt(extension)`) down to 1.5 m/s² and 120°/s.
- **Teleop resets.** The profile resets when the setpoint diverges from the robot by ≥ 0.3 m or ≥ 15°. It also resets when the driver stick passes a threshold.
- **Driver blending.** The output blends toward the driver command with weight `clamp(|stick|·3, 0, 1)`.

#### Shaping the approach ("come in straight")

`AutoScoreCommands.getDriveTarget` [code] keeps moving the target **back along the branch normal** and **sideways**, based on where the robot is in the goal frame. The robot curves around the reef and finishes on the normal. The build thread calls this the 2023 technique of a *"target pose that continuously shifts based on the robot's current position"* [doc].

```java
var offset = robot.relativeTo(goal);
double shiftXT = clamp(|offset.y|/(2*faceLen) + (|offset.x|-0.3)/(4*faceLen), 0, 1);
double shiftYT = clamp(|offset.y| <= 0.2 ? 0 : offset.x/faceLen, 0, 1);
return goal.transformBy(new Transform2d(-shiftXT*1.0, copySign(shiftYT*1.2, offset.y), 0));
```

The goal heading is also "aimed": it points at the branch from the robot, clamped to 0–20° toward the branch side. While the single‑tag blend is ≤ 0.8 in teleop, the goal stays 0.15 m back from the reef (`singleTagTransform`).

#### Local pose: the per‑tag "txTy" pose

Sources: `RobotState.addTxTyObservation`, `getTxTyPose`, `getReefPose` [code]; build thread pages 5 and 15 and post #311 [doc]. **Your description is correct.** Details:

1. **Coprocessor (Northstar, `pipeline/TagAngleCalculator.py`).** For each tag it undistorts the 4 corners and turns them into angles: `tx = atan(K⁻¹·corner).x`, `ty = …y`. **Range** is the norm of the single‑tag IPPE solvePnP translation (the lower‑error solution). The tag's orientation from PnP is not used. [code]
2. **Picking observations.** For each tag, the closest camera's observation wins. Only data newer than what is already stored is kept. [code]
3. **Heading at capture time.** It comes from the pose buffer, not from vision. [code]
   - `robotRotation = estimatedPose.transformBy(new Transform2d(odometryPose, sample)).getRotation()`
   - The estimated heading is gyro plus multi‑tag theta only, because single‑tag theta std dev is ∞.
4. **Geometry.** Mean tx and ty over the 4 corners. Build the camera‑frame vector `(d,0,0)` rotated by `(pitch=ty, yaw=−tx)`. Rotate it by the camera's mount pitch and project it to 2D. [code] Then:

```java
Rotation2d camToTagRot = robotRotation.plus(camPose2d.getRotation().plus(camToTag.getAngle()));
Translation2d fieldToCam = new Pose2d(tagPose2d.getTranslation(), camToTagRot.plus(Rotation2d.kPi))
    .transformBy(toTransform2d(camToTag.getNorm(), 0)).getTranslation();
robotPose = new Pose2d(fieldToCam, robotRotation.plus(camYaw)).transformBy(cameraToRobot); // rot := gyro
```

5. **Frames.** The result is stored in **field coordinates relative to that tag's map pose**. The reef branch goals are built from the same map. So if a real tag is placed off its map position, the error cancels: the robot aligns to the physical tag.
6. **Latency compensation.** `getTxTyPose` applies only the **odometry twist from capture time to now**. The result is dropped if it is older than 0.2 s. [code]

```java
return sample.map(old -> data.pose().plus(new Transform2d(old, odometryPose)));
```

7. **Which tag.** It is the tag of the *target face*, not the nearest tag: blue 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 17 and red 7, 6, 11, 10, 9, 8. These poses are **never fed into the global estimator**, because *"errors in calibration or camera placement are massively amplified"*. [code/doc]
8. **Blending by distance to the goal.**

```java
t = clamp((dist(estimated, finalPose) - 24in) / (36in - 24in), 0, 1);
return new ReefPoseEstimate(estimated.interpolate(tagPose, 1 - t), 1 - t);
```

   The blended pose is passed as `DriveToPose`'s robot supplier. So the controller runs in the blended frame, and the target stays in the map frame. The single‑tag solution is not used far away, because a heading error turns into a large translation error [doc].

#### Timestamps and latency [code]

- **Odometry.** `PhoenixOdometryThread` runs at **250 Hz** using `waitForAll`. Each sample's timestamp is FPGA time minus the mean CAN latency.
- **Pose buffer.** `TimeInterpolatableBuffer<Pose2d>` holding 2 s of odometry‑only poses.
- **Vision fusion** ("rewind, correct, replay"). For each vision measurement:
  - rewind the estimate to the capture time with the odometry delta;
  - apply a diagonal Kalman gain `K = q/(q+√(q·r))`;
  - replay the odometry delta back to now.
- **Vision timestamps.** NT4 value timestamps from Northstar, plus a tunable `AprilTagVision/TimestampOffset` (default 0).
- **Coral (game pieces).** Each piece is ray‑cast from the bottom‑centre tx/ty onto the floor, using the robot pose at capture time.

#### End conditions and "close but not settled" [code]

- **Superstructure may start** when all of these hold:
  - within 2.0 m of the reef;
  - within 35° of the goal heading;
  - within 1.5 m of arc around the reef centre.
- **Eject** requires all of these:
  - blend == 1.0. Teleop only; auto skips this check.
  - Branch‑relative dispenser error within limits, for example L4: |y| ≤ 3 cm and x in [−4, +4] cm.
  - The same check on a **pose predicted 0.3 s ahead** from the current velocity. This stops it scoring while passing through the tolerance.
  - superstructure `atGoal()`;
  - an extra 50 ms debounce at L4.
- **`DriveToPose` never ends by itself.** Callers use `withinTolerance(…)` (for example 5 cm and 5° plus a 0.1 s debouncer for intake). Inside tolerance the velocity is zeroed (1 cm, 1°).

#### Field inaccuracy [code]

- Tag layouts are selected by perimeter type (`welded/`, `andymark/`).
- The default layout is `NO_BARGE`. The reason is not given in what I could read [unverified].
- A dashboard switch selects `BLUE_REEF`/`RED_REEF` (own reef tags only) or `NONE`. Northstar receives the chosen layout over NT.
- Per‑camera `PitchAdjust` tunables.
- Per‑level `branchFudgeX` (L4 +2 cm).
- The main defence is the target‑tag‑relative pose above.

#### Autos [code]

The Championship auto `upInTheAirAuto` has **no pre‑planned paths**. It is `repeatedly()` of:

- `autoScore` (DriveToPose with the superstructure as a deadline);
- `intakeSequence`: DriveToPose to dynamic station or ground poses, then `autoIntake`, which is DriveToPose to the nearest *detected* coral.

Mechanisms run through `deadlineFor` and `raceWith`. The intake starts when the robot is out of the "reef danger" zone.

#### Origin: 2024

`subsystems/drive/controllers/AutoAlignController.java` [code]. Two `ProfiledPIDController`s:

- distance: kP 3.5, accel limited to 0.4× max;
- theta: kP 7.

The distance profile is seeded with `min(0, −v_toward_goal)`. FF is scaled over a radius of 0.2–0.8 m. `atGoal` requires being inside 8 cm and 2° for 0.5 s.

## FRC 254 The Cheesy Poofs (2025 code, the newest public)

### Global drive‑to‑pose: pathfinding plus a stitched "join path"

Sources: `commands/PathfindingAutoAlignCommand.java` and their fork of PathPlanner in `lib/pathplanner/**` [code]; 2025 binder Q&A [doc].

- **Path following.** The robot follows a forked PathPlanner LocalADStar `pathfindThenFollowPath(joinPath, …)`.
  - The **join path** is a straight line along the face normal. It starts at the scoring pose backed off 1.0 m (1.5 m in auto) and ends at the scoring pose. Intermediate waypoints sit at 0.6 of the way (0.2 for "tall"/L4).
  - The A* path is concatenated onto it with `ConcatenationStrategy.SMOOTH`.
  - The binder describes this as stitching a path so the robot *"drive[s] straight into the peg"* [doc].
- **Seeded from current velocity** [code/doc]:
  - `PathfindingCommand.initialize` predicts the pose 30 ms ahead.
  - It passes the field velocity as `startVelocity`.
  - `Waypoint.autoControlFirstPoint` puts the first Bezier control point along `v²·0.1` in the velocity direction.
  - A path cache (up to 120 entries, 0.8 m tolerance) is used only when |v| ≤ 0.05.
  - A `WarmupCommand` pre‑solves paths.
- **Path controller.** Their `PPHolonomicDriveController` fork uses **along‑track (LTE) kP 3** and **cross‑track (CTE) kP 6**, in the path's heading frame. Only along‑track velocity is used as feedforward, plus wheel‑force feedforward. Theta kP is 5. It runs in a **100 Hz `Notifier`** (priority 41), separate from the main loop. [code]
- **Control point.** `setControlPoint(kDriveToCoralOffset = 0.66 m forward)` makes the controller track the coral or claw point, not the robot centre. Rotation feedback is coupled into x and y. [code]
- **Limits.**
  - Pathfinding at 0.8 × 3.6 m/s.
  - The join path is capped at `min(2.0 m/s, 0.6·vmax)` with 0.5× accel.
  - The "tall" robot gets lower separate X and Y accel limits (a fork feature).
- **Simple drive‑to‑pose.** `AutoAlignToPoseCommand` is used only for short relative moves in auto. It is nearly identical to 6328's 2024 controller:
  - distance ProfiledPID kP 5, seeded with `min(0, −v_toward)`;
  - theta ProfiledPID kP 5;
  - FF ramp over 0–0.1 m;
  - ends at 4 cm and 2° (`atGoal`). [code]

### Gating at the stitch point ("close but not settled") [code]

A path callback rewrites constraint zones live:

- **Before ready.** The join path's max speed is set to 0.001 m/s, so the robot effectively waits at the stitch point.
- **When ready.** The real join limits are restored ("Just drive, baby").

The robot is ready when `poseReadyToScore() && stageCommandFinished && doneStaging`:

```java
angle ≤ 6°;  crossTrackErr ≤ 0.10 m (0.15 algae);  |vy| ≤ 0.3;  |ω| ≤ 0.3;
max |pitch/roll rate| over last 0.3 s ≤ 0.3 rad/s;
lastMegatag age ≤ 0.5 s  &&  |megatagPose − pose| ≤ 0.2 m;
if (DriverStation.isAutonomousEnabled()) return true; // "Just send it in auto"
```

The superstructure staging command is placed as an event marker so it fires about 1 s before the stitch point (at an assumed 0.7 × max speed). The superstructure first goes to a `CLEAR_LIMELIGHT` state. The name suggests it keeps the arm out of the camera's view [unverified purpose].

The `PathfindingCommand` ends when trajectory time has elapsed **and** the robot is within 0.2 m. If not within 0.2 m, it gets an extra 0.25 s of grace.

### Local pose: an "exclusive tag" applied to the global estimator [code/doc]

254 does not keep a separate local pose. During alignment they **restrict the global estimator to the target face's tag**:

- `robotState.setExclusiveTag(branch.getBlueTagId()/getRedTagId())`. A MegaTag result is rejected unless its fiducial IDs include that tag.
- Only reef tags are used (`kAprilTagLayoutReefsOnly` = 17–22 and 6–11). [code] In their words: *"We only ever used reef tags, and when scoring we only trusted updates that included the tag on that face."* [doc]

How each Limelight 4's estimate is handled:

- **MegaTag1 is preferred.** Single‑tag MT1 must pass all of these:
  - ambiguity ≤ 0.19;
  - tag area ≥ 1 %;
  - if area < 2 %, |yaw − odometry yaw| ≤ 5°;
  - |z| ≤ 0.2 m.
- **Otherwise `fuseWithGyro`.** It takes **MT1 range and bearing in the robot frame**, re‑rotates them by the **odometry heading at capture time**, and sends theta std dev = 1e6:

```java
Pose2d robotToTag = fieldToTag.relativeTo(mt.fieldToRobot());
Pose2d post = new Pose2d(fieldToTag.getTranslation()
    .minus(robotToTag.getTranslation().rotateBy(prior.getRotation())), prior.getRotation());
```

  Frames are rejected if |yaw rate| > 5 rad/s in the last 0.3 s (time sync is unreliable then). *"MT1 range estimates were excellent but bearing estimates were poor at range"* [doc].
- **Two cameras are combined first.** Both estimates are moved to the same timestamp with the odometry delta, then combined by inverse‑variance weighting. A 2026 GitHub issue from team 4476 reports an asymmetric heading‑fusion bug in this code [doc].
- **Odometry is trusted weakly.** CTRE state std devs are 0.3, 0.3, 0.2 when enabled and 1.0 when disabled. So the estimate snaps quickly to the tag‑relative frame. [code]
- **Relative moves in auto** (sliding from a branch to the algae): the target is `current + (algaePose − branchPose)` **with vision disabled**, so the move is purely by odometry. [code]

### Timestamps [code]

- **CTRE `SwerveDrivetrain`** odometry thread at **250 Hz**, priority 99. The `registerTelemetry` consumer pushes `(RobotTime − Utils.getCurrentTimeSeconds() + state.Timestamp, state.Pose)` into a 1 s `ConcurrentTimeInterpolatableBuffer` keyed by FPGA time.
- **Vision into CTRE.** `addVisionMeasurement(pose, Utils.fpgaToCurrentTime(llTimestamp), stdDevs)`. Std devs come from the Limelight `stddevs` array divided by a quality factor.
- **Extra IMU buffers.** Yaw rate, pitch and roll are buffered too, for the gating checks.

### Field inaccuracy, autos, game pieces, weaknesses

- **Field inaccuracy.** Handled by the exclusive tag plus reef‑only tags. The scoring poses come from the official **welded** map [code]. I found no per‑event calibration.
- **Autos.** `PathfindingAuto` builds steps from a string of letters. Each step is pathfind + join path + staging markers. Obstacle sets switch between auto, teleop and backoff (`Pathfinding.setAutoObstacles()`). [code]
- **Game pieces.** No object detection drives anything; the constants are vestigial. Ice‑cream intake uses fixed poses. [code]
- **Weaknesses.** The heading‑fusion bug above [doc]. In auto, all readiness checks are skipped [code].

## Best ideas to steal

1. **The local pose is a tag‑relative pose expressed in map coordinates** (6328). Range from single‑tag PnP plus tx/ty bearing plus gyro heading at capture time. Then `pose + (odomAtCapture → odomNow)`. Keep it out of the global estimator, and blend it in by distance to the goal (0.6–0.9 m).
2. **Only trust the target's tag while aligning** (both teams). With LimelightLib 2, `SetFiducialIDFiltersOverride` and/or reject frames that do not contain that ID [unverified API name].
3. **Re‑seed the profile every loop from the last setpoint** (6328). Use one 1‑D trapezoid on distance plus a separate theta profile. Fade the FF to zero within 1–5 cm. This handles moving goals and a moving robot cleanly.
4. **Shape the approach by moving the target**, not by planning a path (6328 `getDriveTarget`). Or use a **straight join path along the face normal**, with a gate before it (254).
5. **Gate the final approach on readiness:** heading, cross‑track error, |vy|, |ω|, vision age, vision agreement and mechanism staged (254). Gate scoring on **current *and* velocity‑predicted pose** (6328).
6. **Control a point on the mechanism**, not the robot centre (254's control point).
7. **Chain segments on line crossings or loose tolerances** so the robot does not stop. Fork mechanisms with deadlines and races (6328 2026). In Commands v3 this maps to `fork` plus `await` on a condition.
8. **Odometry is CTRE's 250 Hz thread.** Mirror it into your own FPGA‑time buffer with 254's `registerTelemetry` time conversion, and query poses at capture time from there.

## Pitfalls

- **Single‑tag heading error grows with range.** Use tag poses only up close, and take heading from the gyro (both teams).
- **Never put single‑tag or local poses into the global estimator.** Calibration errors get amplified [doc].
- **Timestamp time bases.** CTRE time is not FPGA time. Convert in both directions (`Utils.fpgaToCurrentTime`), or you get latency‑sized pose errors. Reject vision during fast yaw (254 uses 5 rad/s).
- **Stale data.** Expire local poses after about 0.2 s. Fall back to the global pose *and back the goal off* (6328 uses 0.15 m) instead of driving blind into the target.
- **Robot pose and goal must use the same field map.** A different map for one of them adds the tag placement error.
- **A drive‑to‑pose without an end condition never ends.** Add tolerance plus debounce, a timeout, or crossing checks.
- **Camera dropouts kill auto‑align.** 6328 reported this in 2026. Keep manual or preset fallbacks and log camera health.
- **A path built for a standing start makes a moving robot stop and restart.** 254 rebuilds the first Bezier segment from the actual velocity, and only uses cached paths when |v| ≤ 0.05 m/s.
- **6328's 2026 code assumes a 200 Hz main loop.** On SystemCore you can probably raise the loop rate, but CTRE's swerve still has its own odometry thread. Do not run two estimators that disagree.
