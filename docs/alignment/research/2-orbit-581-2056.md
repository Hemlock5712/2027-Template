## 1. Orbit 1690 (priority)

### What is public
- Orbit publishes **no robot code** [doc]. A 2026 CAD-thread reply says some software stays unpublished, and each year's students write a new clean template.
- The main primary source is the **June 2024 two-part online session**, with slides and a YouTube recording [doc]:
  - Part I (June 16): system control, swerve code structure, tuning, automations, and "swerve algorithm and limits".
  - Part II (June 23): the "PoseTracker", which fuses odometry, AprilTag position and game-piece position, detects skid and collision ("and what to do with both"), and covers shoot-on-the-move.
- I found **no Championship conference talk** and **no 2025 or 2026 session series**. Someone asked about a 2025 repeat in May 2025, and I saw no answer.
- Their 2019 off-season swerve thread and their 2025/2026 reveal-thread replies add details.

### Commanded velocity toward a point
- **2019 architecture** [doc]:
  - One core algorithm takes a **field-frame velocity vector plus ω** and drives all four modules. Teleop and auto both use it.
  - **Three layers of acceleration limits** sit on top:
    1. An omnidirectional limit "for skidding".
    2. Separate limits for each robot axis.
    3. A forward/reverse limit that depends on the robot's **actual** velocity.
  - The trajectory generator respects max velocity, acceleration and **jerk**, and accounts for "velocity lost to rotating the robot".
- **2025** [doc]:
  - Their own path generator builds Bézier curves, then uses kinematic equations to get position and velocity every 20 ms. It exports a large CSV for the robot to follow.
  - In teleop they used "autodrives" to reach the scoring target.
  - The driver picks the target. For the first three coral on a face, the robot goes to the nearest *free* position. After that it goes to the nearest position, and a joystick flick switches targets.
- **`v = min(vmax, sqrt(2·a·d))`**: I could not confirm this exact formula from an Orbit source **[unverified]**. It fits the "simple formulas, command velocity directly" style the team describes, and 581's code (§2) implements exactly this formula, so 581 is a usable reference.
- I found nothing public on Orbit's jerk-limit math beyond the 2019 mention.

### Heading and moving start velocity
- No primary detail is available. The 2019 per-axis and velocity-dependent limits mean the limiter starts from measured velocity rather than zero [doc]. Beyond that, how they seed a moving start is **[unverified]**.

### Skid and slip detection
**The algorithm** [doc, via the "Has anyone successfully implemented Orbit's skid detection" thread and the pramit.gg write-up]:
1. Get the measured chassis ω (from the gyro, or from the modules' inverse kinematics).
2. Use kinematics to build the rotation-only module vectors `ω × r_i`.
3. Subtract them from each measured module vector `v_i`. What remains, `t_i = v_i − ω×r_i`, is the translational part.
4. On a rigid body every `t_i` is the same vector.
5. **Skid ratio = max|t_i| / min|t_i|**, which is always ≥ 1.

Thresholds and behavior:
- Community replies say the ratio is about 1.0 nominally and 1.1–1.2 when driving and rotating at once [doc]. Orbit's own threshold is not public.
- A commenter says Orbit runs **per-module traction control**: each module computes its real velocity along its commanded direction and knows when it is slipping [doc].
- **Response to a skid:** the slides cover "what to do with both", but the content is not visible to me.
  - One writeup that cites Orbit suggests either computing odometry from the 3 non-slipping wheels, or growing (or resetting) the estimator's uncertainty [doc, not Orbit's own words].
  - Lowering odometry trust and leaning on vision during a skid is **[unverified]**.

**Reference implementation, 135-Consul** (`FastSwerve/Swerve.java`) [code]:
- It subtracts `kinematics.toSwerveModuleVelocities(0,0,ω+0.05)` from each module, takes the median of the x and y components, and flags modules more than **0.5 m/s** from the median.
- **It has a bug.** It sorts the component arrays and then indexes them as if they were still per-module, so module identity is lost. It also adds a +0.05 fudge to ω. Don't copy it as-is.

### Collision detection
- Orbit's method is **not public**. The 2024 session listed it as a topic [doc].
- **2026 (newest)** [doc]:
  - Collisions broke Kepler's shoot-on-the-move feedforward. They switched the feedforward to the **wanted (commanded) velocity instead of measured velocity**, to reduce noise.
  - They added a driver-triggered **defense mode** that disables shoot-on-the-move and X-locks the modules.
- The usual FRC pattern is an IMU jerk threshold (§2) **[unverified for Orbit]**.

### Vision fusion and target-relative alignment
- **2024:** the PoseTracker merges odometry, AprilTag pose and game-piece position into one pose [doc].
- **2019:** vision "takes over gradually" once the path brings the robot near the target, in both auto and teleop [doc]. That is a global-to-local blend.
- They use **Arducams** [doc].
- **2026 (newest)** [doc]:
  - The Pigeon drifted about 10° per match, and up to 30° in extreme cases.
  - They compute a **gyro offset from the cameras** (mostly the turret camera) and apply it, so heading stays corrected even when no tags are visible.
  - They disable Pigeon2 **No-Motion Calibration** every time, on CTRE's advice, because vibration can look like drift.
- I found no public detail on any tag-relative "local pose" step.

### Accuracy claims
- I found no numbers for drive-to-point accuracy.
- A Chief Delphi custom-swerve thread estimates Orbit's achieved acceleration at about **10 m/s²** on tread [doc, community estimate].

## 2. 581 Blazing Bulldogs (most code available)

**Repos** [code]:
- `team581/frc-2026` is a monorepo (`shared/`, `comp-bot/`, `beta-bot/`, ...).
- `team581/offseason-2026` is the newest, last pushed 2026-10-10. It is still on GradleRIO 2026, with no 2027 port.
- `2025-beta` is the 2025 Reefscape code.
- `frc-2024-crescendo-gamma` is the 2024 code.
- Libraries: DogLog for logging, CTRE swerve plus pose estimator.

### Drive-to-point: "Trailblazer" (`shared/.../trailblazer`) [code]
**Tracker** (`HeuristicPathTracker`):
- It moves through waypoints, advancing when the robot is inside each point's `PoseErrorTolerance`.
- The target slides along a line or a quadratic Bézier.
- The parameter `t = distFromStart/(distFromStart+distToTarget)` only **ratchets forward**, so vision jumps can't make it jitter.

**Follower** (`PidPathFollower`, `ConstraintsCalculator`):
```java
d = |pose→target| + Σ remaining segment lengths   // full remaining path length
v = |P·d|                                         // P controller on distance-to-end
v = min(v, vmax)                                  // default 4.75 m/s, a = 4 m/s²
v = min(v, max(vCmdPrev + a·dt, 0.5))             // accel limit, 0.5 m/s floor
v = min(v, sqrt(2·a·d))                           // stopping-distance cap
dir = atan2(target.y-y, target.x-x)
return PolarChassisSpeeds(v, dir, ω)
```
- **Heading:** ω comes from a `ProfiledPIDController` (trapezoid, continuous input) when angular constraints are set, and otherwise from a plain PID.
- **Moving start:** on each new segment, `reset()` seeds `vCmdPrev` from |current velocity| and seeds the angular profile from the current ω.

**Newest change (offseason-2026): rotation-aware speed cap** [code]
```java
tRot = max(0, angularWork - angTol) / ωmax
v = min(v, max(d / tRot, 0.4))
if (d <= linTol) v = 0          // wait for rotation instead of orbiting the point
```
- It is applied both per point and per segment.
- It stops the robot arriving translationally before it has finished rotating, which caused oscillation.
- The "trustworthy" threshold also rose from 5 in to 10 in.

### Alignment (2025 reef)
**`TagAlign.getPoseAlignmentChassisSpeeds`** [code]:
- It rotates the field error into the **scoring pose's frame**.
- It applies separate P gains: sideways P = 5.0 and forward P = 4.5.
- It rotates the result back to the field frame. Heading is handled separately.
- "Aligned" means within **3 cm and 5°**, debounced for 0.15 s.

**Local-pose trick: `CLOSEST_REEF_TAG` state** [code]:
- While aligning, every Limelight's `SetFiducialIDFiltersOverride` is set to the **single tag on the face being scored**.
- MT2 xy std dev is `0.01·d^1.2`.
- So the pose estimator gets pulled toward that one tag, and that face's placement error mostly drops out of the global pose. It's a cheap way to get target-relative accuracy while staying inside the global pose.

**`FieldCalibrationUtil`** [code]:
- A disabled-mode feature flag that logs every scoring pose and lights LEDs when the robot, placed by hand at a pipe, matches within **5 cm and 1°**.
- It is used to check the field at events.

### Localization and vision fusion (2026, newest)
**Estimator and timing** [code]:
- CTRE's estimator is used directly.
- Odometry std devs are `(0.01, 0.01, 0.002)`.
- `addVisionMeasurement(pose, fpgaToCurrentTime(min(now−k, ts−k)), std)` takes a tunable static-latency term k.

**Limelight std devs** [code]:
- xy: `0.01·d^0.8`, plus a velocity term above 2 m/s (its multiplier defaults to 0).
- θ: 999 (ignored), except MT1 rotation is used within 40 in, with θ std `0.03·d^1.2`.

**`PoseEstimateValidator` rejects a frame when** [code]:
- |ω| > 100°/s
- tagCount == 0
- a single tag has ambiguity ≥ 0.7
- the pose is (0,0) (Limelight brown-out)
- an MT2 pose's heading is more than 45° off the gyro
- the frame is a duplicate (per-source timestamps)

**`TrustFactor` (pose confidence)** [code]:
```text
trust += distanceTravelled · odomStd                  // grows with distance
trust += 2.0 on collision
on vision: trust = |odomPose(t_capture) − stdWeightedVisionMean| + weighted std
```
- "Lost" means ≥ 1.0. "Trustworthy" means ≤ 10 in.
- Autos branch on it.

**Collision** (`Imu.java`) [code]:
- It takes a 10-sample moving average of Pigeon x and y acceleration and differentiates it to get jerk.
- It flags a collision when `|jerk|/9.81 > 0.8`.
- **Units are mislabeled:** the Pigeon reports acceleration in g, so the threshold is really about 7.8 g/s.

**Bump crossing** [code]:
- A pitch/roll state machine (flat → uphill → downhill → flat, with timeouts) **resets the translation to a known landing point** after crossing the 2026 bump.
- It is a deliberate "odometry is wrong now, re-seed it" event.

**Skid:** 581 has no skid detector, only CTRE's slip-current limit of 120 A [code].

### Target-relative and capture-time ideas
- **2024 speaker aim:**
  - Distance comes from `ty` geometry.
  - Aim angle is `gyroHeading(t_capture) − tx`, where `t_capture = now − (capture + pipeline latency)`. Heading is read from a history at the capture time [code].
  - This is pure local alignment with only a heading twist.
- **2026 game pieces:**
  - Field position = `samplePoseAt(t_capture)` ∘ robot-relative tx/ty observation [code].
  - The robot then drives with its current pose. Pose error shared by both cancels, leaving only the odometry twist since capture, which is exactly the compensation your team wants.

## 3. 2056 OP Robotics

**Public code:** `github.com/Team2056/PublicCodeBank` has branches `2025` and `2026` [code].
- It is C++, **drivebase plus odometry plus Limelight only**. There is no superstructure and no real autos (2026 ships only `DriveStraight`).
- It is custom. It does not use CTRE swerve or WPILib kinematics, and it works in inches.

### Drive-to-point (`OPRDriveBase::DriveToPoint`, the same in 2025 and 2026) [code]
```cpp
diff = point - odomPose;                         // vector
mag  = continuous ? maxVel : -MAX_SPEED*DrivePID(|diff|);  // P=0.025 → 4.5·d in/s
mag  = min(mag, maxVel);
vx = mag*cos(diff.angle); vy = mag*sin(diff.angle);
ω  = MAX_ROT*TurnPID(gyro → point.angle), clamped to ±maxRVel
FieldCentricDrive(vx, vy, ω, robotYComp);
```
- **Limiter:** the field-centric drive slew-limits **field x and y separately**: 600 in/s² (about 15 m/s²) with a "jerk" ramp of 20000 in/s³. ω is limited separately.
- **"Continuous" mode** commands max speed and switches to the next point when within tolerance. That gives pass-through waypoints with no stop [code, and confirmed in binder replies [doc]].
- **Skew compensation:** it adds a perpendicular term `0.65·(0.5|v|·ω)` at −90° to the robot-frame velocity, keeping the magnitude, to cancel the arc you get when translating and rotating at once.
- **`robotYComp`:** a lateral bias used to track game pieces [code]. In teleop it is driver speed times a coral `tx` correction [doc].

### Odometry [code]
- Each module is integrated as a field-frame point.
- The center is computed from each wheel, then the four centers are averaged.
- Then `RefitGeometry` snaps the module points back to rigid geometry.
- It uses Phoenix Pro time sync (`WaitForAll(15ms)`) [code and doc].
- There is **no Kalman fusion**, **no skid or collision logic**, and velocity comes from differencing poses.

### Vision and the local approach
**Public code** [code]:
- No MegaTag.
- Distance comes from a **measured `ty` → distance interpolation table**, with separate practice-bot and comp-bot tables. Angle comes from `tx`.
- **2025 `ResetOdometryPos(offset)`** hard-resets odometry into a tag-centered frame: `x = (dist+18.25)·cos(gyro−tx) − offset.x`, and the same for y.
- **2026 `RecalculateOdometryPos`** sets the pose to `tagTable[tid] + targetVector.rotate(gyro)`.
- Neither has latency compensation.

**2025 binder replies** [doc]:
- For L2–L4 they measured the scoring spot **relative to the chassis and added that offset to the current odometry estimate**. That is a true local pose with an odometry twist.
- L1 used a field-relative reset ("MegaTag at home").
- In auto, the only vision update was snapping to the known pole after scoring.
- The 2023 binder says they compensated for *repeatable* drift in commanded positions rather than chasing perfect odometry.

**Accuracy:** none published. Default `DriveToPoint` tolerance is 10 in [code].

## Best ideas to steal
1. **The kinematic speed law from 581's `ConstraintsCalculator`:** `v = min(P·d, vmax, vPrev + a·dt, sqrt(2·a·d))`, pointed along the direction to the target, with `d` as the *remaining path length*. Seed `vPrev` from the measured speed when a segment starts. This is the "simple formula" in proven code.
2. **The rotation-aware cap from 581's offseason-2026:** `v ≤ d / (angleLeft/ωmax)`, and command zero translation once inside the linear tolerance while rotation finishes. It stops end-of-move orbiting.
3. **A ratchet-only progress parameter** for a sliding target, so vision jumps never pull the setpoint backward.
4. **Do local alignment in the target's frame.** Rotate the error into the tag frame and use separate lateral and forward gains (581 2025). Lateral error is what matters for scoring.
5. **Capture-time compensation.** `target_field = samplePoseAt(t_capture) ∘ robotRelativeObservation`, then servo with the current pose. Shared global error cancels and only odometry drift since capture remains. CTRE's `samplePoseAt` already gives you the history.
6. **Single-tag filtering during the final approach** (581 `CLOSEST_REEF_TAG`). Fuse only the target's tag, with tight std devs. It is almost free with LimelightLib 2's `SetFiducialIDFiltersOverride`.
7. **Orbit's skid ratio** `max|t_i|/min|t_i|`, with `t_i = v_i − ω×r_i`. Use the gyro ω, and on skid inflate the odometry std dev (or drop the worst module). Write your own version; don't copy 135's buggy one.
8. **A TrustFactor-style confidence number** that grows with distance and collisions and is reset by vision agreement. Gate autos and alignment on it.
9. **A strict vision validator:** reject on |ω|, single-tag ambiguity, a (0,0) pose, MT2 heading disagreement, and duplicate timestamps.
10. **Orbit 2026:** feed shoot-on-the-move and other feedforwards with *commanded* velocity, disable Pigeon2 No-Motion Calibration, and correct gyro drift from vision.
11. **A field-calibration mode** that checks scoring poses against the real field at events.

## Pitfalls
- **Per-axis slew limits (2056)** change the direction of travel during acceleration. Limit the velocity *vector* magnitude (581's polar approach) instead.
- **A plain P on distance (2056)** has no stopping-distance cap, so it overshoots at high gain. Add the `sqrt(2ad)` term.
- **581's 0.5 m/s acceleration floor** lets the command jump to 0.5 m/s immediately, and its `sqrt` cap ignores the remaining heading change. Watch both on short moves.
- **Hard odometry resets from tx/ty without latency compensation (2056)** introduce lag error while moving. Use capture-time sampling.
- **Unit mistakes in collision detection.** Pigeon acceleration is in g, and 581's "GForce" is actually jerk. Write down the units of any threshold you copy.
- **A skid ratio is noisy at low speed** because min|t_i| goes to 0. Gate it on speed, or use differences (|t_i − median|) like 135, but keep module identity.
- **Trusting the MT2 heading.** 581 throws it away (θ std 999) and only uses MT1 rotation close up.
- **Orbit details beyond the 2024 skid ratio and the 2026 forum replies are unverified.** Check the 2024 session video (the skid section is around 28 minutes in) before treating any Orbit formula as authoritative.
